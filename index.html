Game state and data generation

App. AApp. E.2App. E.3Fig. 6

A game state has 117 dimensions. Most hold a 3D position or a velocity. These clips draw the state on a plan view of the arena. A short trail follows each car. No model made these clips. Two sources appear here. The Rocket Science dataset (RoSc) holds the recordings. RoLeSynth is a rule-based engine, built with LLM assistance and fitted to those recordings. A match is configured by the fitted physics, four player profiles of 103 behaviour parameters each, and a team assignment. RoLeSynth simulates a 5-minute match in about 1.4 s on one CPU core, which is about 220 times real time. It does not take actions as input. It reads them off the motion of each tick, using the vocabulary of 19 binary actions from Rocket Science.

Dual-stream world model

Sec. 3.2Fig. 2

DSWM denoises the state stream and the video stream together. It receives no state as input here. It receives the actions. It generates the state and the video at the same time. In the side-by-side clips the video is on the left and the state the model generated is on the right. That state is model output. It was not given to the model. The overlay shows the actions for each frame. The wheel shows the steering direction. The lights show jump and boost.

Uncertainty over hidden state

Sec. 4.3Fig. 5App. H

DSWM draws its flow-matching noise for each stream independently. This permits a third inference mode. Teacher-force the video, then predict the state. Ten samples from one video give a confidence estimate for each state dimension. The spread is about 35 times wider for cars the camera does not show. The ratio of error to spread is 0.96 to 1.10 for controlled, visible and hidden cars alike. In each clip the state view is above and the video is below. Every faint marker is one of the ten samples.

Novel actions

App. F.1App. E.3Sec. 4.3

MAGNET and HOVER are two new actions. The Rocket Science recordings contain neither. RoLeSynth adds them as short plug-in rules, as two new actions beside the 19 binary actions of Rocket Science. MAGNET pulls the ball toward the car for 1 s. HOVER holds the car at a fixed altitude for a few seconds. A new ability takes three small files in RoLeSynth, against 11 to 12 files in RocketSim. In each clip the generated video is on the left and the state view is on the right. Read the state view like this. Each car is a triangle pointing the way it faces, blue for one team and orange for the other. The ball is the white circle. A purple ring around a car means one of its ability fields is active, so a ringed car is using MAGNET or HOVER at that moment. The panel on the far right lists each car, its boost level, and its ability fields in purple, for example IS_USING_MAGNET, MAGNET_COOLDOWN and MAGNET_TIME. The strip along the bottom is a side view of the arena, which is where HOVER is easiest to see.
